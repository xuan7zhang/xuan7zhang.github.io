I am a Ph.D. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of British Columbia, advised by <a href="https://tea.ece.ubc.ca/">Prof. Xiaoxiao Li</a>. My research is driven by one idea: find the low-dimensional structure that actually governs a model's behavior, and act on that structure rather than on the whole system. Doing so makes learning more efficient, more interpretable, and easier to control.

Current themes include:

- targeted intervention in large models: locating where undesirable behaviors such as memorization live in internal representations, and correcting them selectively without sacrificing general capability;
- principled self-improvement: letting models and agents refine their own components, such as the tools they use, from likelihood-based signals rather than heuristics or manual curation;
- structured and interpretable learning: low-rank and sparse decompositions, and deep unfolding networks that turn classical optimization into interpretable architectures.

This thread runs from my earlier work on multi-view learning, image restoration, and 3D vision at the University of Ottawa and Harbin Institute of Technology, Shenzhen, to my current work on large language models and agents at UBC.

I am always happy to connect about research collaborations. Feel free to reach out by email.
